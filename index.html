04 — PHARMACY TOOLS

Practical calculators and medicine-safety utilities.

A growing collection of lightweight tools for pharmacists, students and healthcare professionals. They are educational aids—not substitutes for clinical judgment, current references or patient-specific prescribing decisions.

CrCl

Creatinine clearance

Cockcroft–Gault estimate

Enter values to calculate an estimate.

Formula: Cockcroft–Gault: [(140 − age) × weight] ÷ [72 × serum creatinine in mg/dL], multiplied by 0.85 for women. The calculator uses the weight you enter; it does not select actual, ideal or adjusted body weight for you. Choose the appropriate weight according to your local clinical guidance. µmol/L is converted to mg/dL by dividing by 88.4. Reference: Cockcroft DW, Gault MH. Nephron. 1976;16:31–41.

BMI

BMI calculator

Weight and height

Enter weight and height.

Formula: weight (kg) ÷ height (m)². Adult BMI categories follow WHO classification; BMI is a screening measure, not a direct measure of body fat or an individual diagnosis. Not intended for children or pregnancy.

IV

Infusion rate

Volume over time

Enter volume and time.

Formula: infusion rate (mL/hour) = total volume (mL) ÷ time (hours). This does not calculate drops/minute; a giving-set drop factor is required for that.

D/V

Dose → volume

Liquid medicine calculation

Enter dose, strength and volume.

Formula: volume required = (prescribed dose ÷ available dose) × available volume. Confirm that dose and strength use the same units and verify the product concentration before administration.

DDI

Quick interaction screen

High-yield flags for common combinations

This screen is intentionally limited and is not a comprehensive interaction database.

This is a small, manually curated screening set, not a comprehensive interaction database. An unflagged pair must never be interpreted as safe. Verify every result against a current professional reference, the patient's full medication list and clinical context. FDA interaction guidance ↗

Clinical safety note: calculations are mathematical estimates. Verify units, patient factors, reference equations and local guidelines before using a result in practice.

06 — RESEARCH & PUBLICATIONS

Contributing to evidence that informs medicine safety.

My published research contribution is in preclinical pharmacology and developmental toxicology. The article is open access and can be read in full.

ORIGINAL RESEARCH ARTICLE9 FEBRUARY 2026

Prenatal Developmental Toxicity of ARVIDEX® Herbal Medicine in Albino Wistar Rats

Ogwang PE, Joshua K, Katende P, et al. Cureus. 2026;18(2):e103279.

This preclinical study evaluated the potential prenatal developmental toxicity of ARVIDEX®, a polyherbal formulation, in pregnant albino Wistar rats. Fifty pregnant rats were allocated to five groups and received different oral dose levels or control treatment during gestation. Maternal and fetal outcomes were assessed.

Study focusMaternal and fetal outcomes, including maternal weight and food intake, pregnancy parameters and fetal development.
Key findingDose-related adverse maternal and developmental effects were reported at moderate-to-high doses. No statistically significant adverse effects were observed for the measured endpoints at the human-equivalent dose in this animal model.
My authorship contributionThe published author-contribution statement credits me with concept and design; acquisition, analysis or interpretation of data; and drafting the manuscript.

This is preclinical animal research. Its findings should not be interpreted as proof of safety or efficacy in human pregnancy.
